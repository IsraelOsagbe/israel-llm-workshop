Tavares Breakage Model Parameters for Sand & Ceramic Proppants

Calibration for ANSYS Rocky DEM to Match Hu Sun et al. (2022) Fracture Conductivity Results

Overview & Parameters Hypothesis & Research Gap

Background

The Tavares breakage model (Tavares et al., 2020, Powder Technology 362:208–220) is implemented in ANSYS Rocky DEM v4.3+ for particle breakage simulation. It requires 17 parameters governing:

  • Breakage probability: Size-dependent fracture energy distribution (E∞, d₀, φ, σ)
  • Damage accumulation: Weakening by repeated sub-critical stressing (γ)
  • Fragment size distribution: Energy-dependent progeny distribution (A, b′, αₙ/βₙ for n=1.2, 1.5, 2, 4, 25, 50, 75)

This work calibrates the model for quartz sand and ceramic proppants (CarboAir, CarboLite) to match the experimental fracture conductivity and breakage behavior published by Hu Sun et al. (2022) in ACS Omega 7:10243–10254.

⚠️ Key Finding: No Published Parameters Exist

Critical limitation: No Tavares model parameters have been published for any proppant material. All values below are assumed from surrogate materials in Tavares et al. (2020):

  • Ceramics (CarboAir, CarboLite) → Assumed from limestone (weakest material in Tavares et al., Table 2)
  • Quartz sand → Assumed from copper ore (strongest material in Tavares et al., Table 2)

This is documented as Assumption in the parameter table. See Hypothesis page for why this matters and the research gap it creates.

Hu Sun's Empirical Conductivity Model (Eqs. 12–13)

20/40 mesh: kWf = (32.344·PS – 109.371)·ln(σ) + (203.87·PS – 518.86)

40/70 mesh: kWf = (5.8535·PS – 25.844)·ln(σ) + (43.574·PS – 123.28)

Where kWf = conductivity (μm²·cm), σ = closure stress (MPa), PS = sand volume proportion.
