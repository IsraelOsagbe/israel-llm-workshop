Hypotheses Tested & Research Gap

Why the Tavares model parameters don't transfer to proppants — and what to do about it

Overview & Parameters Hypothesis & Research Gap

Six Hypotheses Tested and Rejected

H1: Limestone parameters work for CarboAir/CarboLite ceramics

❌ REJECTED

Reason: Limestone (E∞=7 J/kg) is a weak sedimentary rock failing by cleavage along bedding planes. CarboAir (ultra-low-density ceramic, SG=2.0) and CarboLite (low-density ceramic, SG=2.71) are engineered ceramics failing by transgranular fracture. Different Weibull modulus, different fracture mechanics.

But: No alternative exists in literature. Limestone values used as lower-bound placeholder with explicit assumption flag.

H2: Copper ore parameters work for quartz sand

❌ REJECTED

Reason: Copper ore (E∞=213.5 J/kg) is a high-strength crystalline material. Quartz frac sand has characteristic strength ~180 MPa (Man & Wong, 2017) vs. copper ore's much higher strength. Size-effect exponent φ=1.22 for copper ore vs. unknown for sand.

But: No published Tavares parameters exist for frac sand. Copper ore values serve as upper-bound placeholder.

H3: CarboAir and CarboLite can share parameters

❌ REJECTED

Reason: CarboAir (SG=2.0, bulk density=1.15 g/cm³) is ultra-low-density with engineered internal porosity. CarboLite (SG=2.71, bulk density=1.57 g/cm³) is low-density with different microstructure. Crush resistance differs: CarboLite 40/70 K-value=17K psi (Sutradhor & Ghassemi, 2025); CarboAir 40/70 shows 12% fines at 8,000 psi (CARBOAIR TDS).

But: No Tavares parameters exist for either. Both currently assigned limestone values.

H4: Hu Sun et al. (2022) ceramics are CarboAir or CarboLite

❌ REJECTED

Reason: Hu Sun et al. state: "ceramic and quartz sand proppant obtained from Changqing oilfield, China" (p. 2). These are Chinese-manufactured ceramics, not CARBO Ceramics products. Hu Sun ceramic bulk density = 1.46/1.43 g/cm³ vs. CarboAir 1.15 and CarboLite 1.57.

Implication: CarboAir/CarboLite TDS data used only as representative properties for simulation setup.

H5: Tavares size function captures stronger size effect in sand vs. ceramic

⚠️ PARTIALLY SUPPORTED BUT UNVERIFIED

Evidence: Hu Sun shows 20/40 sand Br=45.68% vs. 40/70 sand Br=30.39% (1.5× difference). Ceramic: 21.22% vs. 12.3% (1.7× difference). Man & Wong (2017) confirm size effect in single-grain tests.

Problem: Tavares size function E₅₀ = E∞ / (1 + (d/d₀)^φ) with d₀=8.07 mm (copper ore) or 100 mm (limestone). Proppants are 0.2–0.4 mm. At d ≪ d₀, E₅₀ ≈ E∞ (constant). The model predicts NO size effect at proppant scale.

H6: Damage parameter γ calibrates from Mark et al. (2022) 250-day decline

❌ REJECTED

Reason: Mark et al. measure time-dependent conductivity decline (LCDF = 0.034–0.186 md-ft/ln(hr)) under constant stress. Tavares γ governs damage from repeated sub-critical impacts (Eq. 12: D = 2γ/(2γ-5) · (Eₖ/E)^(2γ/5)). Different physics: static fatigue vs. impact damage accumulation. No direct mapping exists.

The Research Gap: Next-Generation Idea

Core Problem: Scale Mismatch in the Tavares Size Function

The Tavares model was developed for comminution (particles 1–100 mm). Its size function:

E₅₀ = E∞ / (1 + (d/d₀)^φ)

with d₀ ≈ 1–100 mm (Table 2: copper ore d₀=8.07 mm, limestone d₀=100 mm).

Proppants are 0.2–0.4 mm (200–400 μm). At d/d₀ ≈ 0.002–0.04, the size function is essentially flat (E₅₀ ≈ E∞). The model predicts no size effect at proppant scale, but experiments clearly show a strong size effect: smaller particles (40/70) have lower breakage rates than larger (20/40) for both sand and ceramic.

Why This Matters for ANSYS Rocky Simulation

In Rocky DEM, every particle gets a random fracture energy from the lognormal distribution. If E₅₀ is constant across 20/40 and 40/70 sizes, the simulation cannot reproduce the observed size-dependent breakage rates without ad hoc per-size-class parameter tuning.

Next-Generation Idea: Dual-Mechanism Size Function for Proppants

Develop a modified size function for the Tavares model that activates at 0.1–1 mm scale:

E₅₀(d) = E∞ / [1 + (d/d₀)^φ]  ×  [1 + ψ·exp(-d/δ)]   for d < 1 mm

Where:

  • First term: Original Tavares function (comminution scale, d₀ ~ 10 mm)
  • Second term: Proppant-scale correction (ψ, δ fitted to single-grain diametrical compression data)
  • ψ: Amplitude of proppant-scale size effect
  • δ: Characteristic length (~100–300 μm) where surface-flaw statistics transition to volume-flaw statistics

Calibration Path (Actionable)

  1. Use Man & Wong (2017) single-grain data: failure force vs. grain height for Baylic Sand, Hydroprop, OxSteel, SLB (10–13 grains per type, already measured)
  2. Fit ψ and δ for each material class (sand, low-density ceramic, high-density ceramic)
  3. Implement in Rocky DEM via user-defined breakage model or parameter override
  4. Validate against Hu Sun et al. (2022) breakage rates at 70 MPa for 20/40 vs. 40/70

Impact

This would enable Rocky DEM simulations to natively capture the 20/40 vs. 40/70 breakage rate differences observed in Hu Sun et al. (2022) without manual parameter tuning per size class — a fundamental improvement for proppant-scale DEM.

Why I Stopped Here

  1. Exhausted all available sources: Read all 16 papers in mine/ including primary Tavares model papers (Tavares & King, 1998; Tavares et al., 2020; Tavares, 2022 KONA review), target experimental paper (Hu Sun et al., 2022), proppant TDS sheets (CarboAir, CarboLite), and analogous mechanical studies (Man & Wong, 2017; Mark et al., 2022; Sutradhor & Ghassemi, 2025).
  2. Fundamental gap is documented: No Tavares parameters exist for proppants in literature. The model was designed for comminution (mm–cm scale), not proppant packs (μm–mm scale).
  3. All measurable calibration targets captured: Hu Sun et al. (2022) provides conductivity vs. stress, breakage rate vs. PS, and empirical prediction model. Man & Wong (2017) provides single-grain and pack properties. Mark et al. (2022) provides long-term conductivity decline. Sutradhor & Ghassemi (2025) provides crush resistance for CarboLite (RC).
  4. Research gap is actionable: The dual-mechanism size function proposal is specific, testable with existing data (Man & Wong single-grain tests), and would directly improve Rocky DEM simulations for proppants.
  5. Further reading would only find comminution-scale data: Searching for "Tavares model proppant" or "Rocky DEM proppant breakage parameters" returns the same Tavares et al. (2020) paper and marketing materials—no proppant-specific parameters.
