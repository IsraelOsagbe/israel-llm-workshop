About This Solver

This page solves the Fanno flow problem: steady, adiabatic flow of a compressible fluid through a constant-area duct with friction. It corresponds to the "Compressible Channel Example" from Lesson 2 (air at 300 m/s, Ma ≈ 0.86 in a 5 cm pipe).

Fanno flow is characterized by:

  • Constant stagnation temperature (adiabatic, no work)
  • Entropy increases due to friction
  • Mach number approaches 1 (choking) as duct length increases
  • Subsonic flow accelerates; supersonic flow decelerates

Fanno Flow Governing Equations

For steady, 1D, adiabatic flow in a constant-area duct with wall friction:

Continuity: ρu = G = constant Momentum: dp + ρu du + (f/2D) ρu² dx = 0 Energy: h₀ = h + u²/2 = constant (stagnation enthalpy) Equation of State: p = ρRT

Dimensionless Form (Fanno Relations)

fL*/D = (1-Ma²)/(γ·Ma²) + (γ+1)/(2γ) · ln[ (γ+1)Ma² / (2(1 + (γ-1)/2·Ma²)) ] T/T* = (γ+1) / (2 + (γ-1)Ma²) p/p* = 1/Ma · √[ (γ+1) / (2 + (γ-1)Ma²) ] ρ/ρ* = 1/Ma · √[ (2 + (γ-1)Ma²) / (γ+1) ] u/u* = Ma · √[ (γ+1) / (2 + (γ-1)Ma²) ] p₀/p₀* = (1/Ma) · [ (γ+1) / (2 + (γ-1)Ma²) ]^((γ+1)/(2(γ-1)))

Star (*) denotes sonic (Ma=1) reference conditions. L* is the duct length required to choke the flow from the given Mach number.

Key Physics

  • Choking: When fL/D ≥ fL*/D, the flow chokes at the exit (Ma=1). Further length increase reduces mass flow rate.
  • Subsonic branch: Friction accelerates flow (Ma ↑), decreases pressure & density, increases velocity.
  • Supersonic branch: Friction decelerates flow (Ma ↓), increases pressure & density, decreases velocity.
  • Second Law: Entropy always increases (ds > 0), stagnation pressure always decreases (dp₀ < 0).
